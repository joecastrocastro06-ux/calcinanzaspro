How it works

What this calculator does

This calculator shows the final price after one or two stacked discounts, how much you save and the real, effective discount you’re getting. You can also add sales tax to see what you’ll actually pay at the register.

The formula

Price after 1st discount = price × (1 − D1 ÷ 100)
Price after 2nd discount = that × (1 − D2 ÷ 100)
Savings = price − price after discounts
Effective discount (%) = savings ÷ price × 100
Final price = price after discounts × (1 + tax ÷ 100)

Worked example, step by step

A jacket costs $80.00. It’s 25% off, and you have a coupon for an extra 10% off the sale price.

  1. After the first discount: $80.00 × (1 − 0.25) = $60.00
  2. After the extra discount: $60.00 × (1 − 0.10) = $54.00
  3. Total saved: $80.00 − $54.00 = $26.00
  4. Effective discount: $26.00 ÷ $80.00 × 100 = 32.5% — not 35%, because the second discount applies to the reduced price.

Frequently asked questions

Do two discounts add up? Is 25% + 10% the same as 35%?

No. The second discount applies to the already reduced price, so 25% then 10% equals 32.5% off, not 35%.

Does the order of the discounts matter?

No. Multiplication is commutative: 10% then 25% gives exactly the same final price as 25% then 10%.

Is sales tax charged before or after the discount?

In most US states, sales tax is calculated on the discounted price, which is how this calculator works. Some manufacturer coupons are taxed on the original price, so check your receipt.

How do I find the original price from a sale price?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount). A $54 item at 25% off was originally $54 ÷ 0.75 = $72.
