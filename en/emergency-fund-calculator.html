How it works

What this calculator does

This calculator sizes your emergency fund: the cash cushion that keeps you afloat if you lose your income or face a big unexpected bill. It adds up your essential monthly costs, multiplies them by the months of cover you want and tells you how long it will take to get there.

The formula

Monthly essentials = housing + food + utilities + transport + health
Fund target = monthly essentials × months of cover
Shortfall = max(0, target − current savings)
Months to complete = shortfall ÷ monthly saving (rounded up)

Worked example, step by step

Your essential monthly costs are: housing $1,200.00, food $500.00, utilities $200.00, transport $150.00 and health $150.00. You want 6 months of cover, have $3,000.00 saved and can set aside $500.00 a month.

  1. Monthly essentials: 1,200 + 500 + 200 + 150 + 150 = $2,200.00
  2. Fund target: $2,200.00 × 6 = $13,200.00
  3. Still missing: $13,200.00 − $3,000.00 = $10,200.00
  4. Time to complete: $10,200.00 ÷ $500.00 = 20.4 → 21 months (1 year and 9 months)

Frequently asked questions

How many months of expenses should I save?

Three months is a common minimum, six is the usual recommendation, and nine to twelve makes sense if your income is irregular, you’re self-employed, a single earner or work in an unstable industry.

Which expenses count as essential?

Only what you’d still have to pay in a crisis: rent or mortgage, food, utilities, insurance, transport to work, minimum debt payments and medicine. Leave out dining out, subscriptions and travel.

Where should I keep my emergency fund?

Somewhere safe and easy to access within a day or two, such as a high-yield savings or money market account. It shouldn’t be invested in stocks, which can fall exactly when you need the money.

Should I pay off debt or build the fund first?

A common approach is to build a small starter fund (one month of expenses) first, then attack high-interest debt, and then complete the full fund.
