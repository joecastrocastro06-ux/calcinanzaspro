How it works

What this calculator does

This page bundles the three percentage questions people ask most: what is X% of a number, what percentage one number is of another, and how much a value went up or down in percent. Each result updates as you type and has its own copy button.

The formula

X% of Y = (X ÷ 100) × Y
X as a % of Y = (X ÷ Y) × 100
Percentage change from X to Y = (Y − X) ÷ |X| × 100

Worked example, step by step

  1. What is 15% of 80? (15 ÷ 100) × 80 = 12
  2. What percentage is 12 of 80? (12 ÷ 80) × 100 = 15%
  3. Change from 80 to 92: (92 − 80) ÷ |80| × 100 = +15%, an increase. Going back from 92 to 80 is (80 − 92) ÷ 92 × 100 = −13.04%, not −15%, because the starting value is different.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number in my head?

Find 10% by moving the decimal point one place left, then scale. 15% of 80 is 10% (8) plus half of that (4), so 12.

Why isn’t a 20% rise followed by a 20% drop back to the start?

Because the second percentage applies to a bigger number. 100 + 20% = 120, and 120 − 20% = 96. Percentage changes don’t cancel out.

What’s the difference between percent and percentage points?

If a rate goes from 4% to 5%, it rose by 1 percentage point, but by 25% in relative terms. Use points for the difference between two percentages.

Can a percentage be more than 100%?

Yes. 150 is 150% of 100, and growing from 50 to 200 is a 300% increase. Only shares of a whole, such as a slice of a pie, are capped at 100%.
