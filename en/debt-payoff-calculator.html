How it works

Both methods work the same way: you pay the minimum on every debt, then put all your extra money on one “target” debt. They only differ in which debt is the target.

  1. Snowball targets the debt with the smallest balance first. You clear debts quickly, which keeps you motivated.
  2. Avalanche targets the debt with the highest interest rate first. It usually costs less interest overall.
What is rollover? When a debt is paid off, you don’t spend its payment on something else. You add it to the next target debt. Your total monthly payment never changes, but it gets concentrated on fewer debts, so each one disappears faster. That’s the “snowball” effect.

A simple example

You owe two debts and can pay $250 per month in total: minimums of $50 and $100, plus $100 extra. To keep the numbers simple, interest is ignored here.

Target debtDebt A (smallest balance: $500)Debt B (highest rate: 24%)

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, snowball or avalanche?

Avalanche usually saves the most interest because it attacks the highest rate first. Snowball clears small balances sooner, which keeps many people motivated. The best method is the one you will stick with.

Do I still pay the minimum on every debt?

Yes. Both methods pay the minimum on every debt each month and send all extra money to one target debt. Missing minimums can bring fees and hurt your credit score.

What happens when a debt is paid off?

Its payment rolls over to the next target debt. Your total monthly payment stays the same, but it is concentrated on fewer debts, so each one disappears faster.

Is it worth paying more than the minimum?

Almost always. Even a small extra payment can cut years off your payoff date and save a large share of the interest, as the comparison table shows.

The math behind it: each debt’s monthly rate is i = r/12 (nominal) or (1 + r)^(1/12) − 1 (effective). Your total monthly budget is S = sum of minimum payments + extra payment, and it stays constant. Each month, interest is added, every debt receives its minimum payment, and whatever remains of S goes to the priority debt. When a debt is cleared, its minimum payment rolls over to the next one. Snowball prioritizes the smallest starting balance; avalanche prioritizes the highest interest rate. “Minimums only” pays just the minimums, with no extra and no rollover.
