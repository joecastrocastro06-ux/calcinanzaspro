How it works

What this calculator does

This calculator works out how much you need to save every month to reach a savings goal by a set date, taking into account what you’ve already saved and the interest your money earns along the way. It also estimates when you’ll hit 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% of the goal.

The formula
i = annual rate ÷ 12, N = months
Monthly saving = [Goal − Current·(1 + i)N] · i ÷ [(1 + i)N − 1]
(with 0% interest: (Goal − Current) ÷ N)
Weekly equivalent = monthly × 12 ÷ 52
Worked example, step by step

You want $20,000.00 in 3 years for a home down payment. You already have $2,000.00 and your savings account pays 5% a year, compounded monthly.

  1. Monthly rate i = 5% ÷ 12 = 0.4167%, N = 36 months; (1 + i)36 = 1.1615
  2. Your $2,000.00 grows on its own to $2,000.00 × 1.1615 = $2,322.94
  3. Gap to cover with deposits: $20,000.00 − $2,322.94 = $17,677.06
  4. Monthly saving: $17,677.06 × i ÷ [(1 + i)36 − 1] = $456.14 (about $105.26 a week)
  5. You put in $16,421.14 out of pocket and interest adds $1,578.86.

Frequently asked questions

What if my current savings already cover the goal?

If what you have will grow to the goal on its own, the required monthly saving is zero. The milestones table then shows when interest alone gets you there.

What return should I assume?

Use a conservative rate for short goals (under 3–5 years), such as what a high-yield savings account or CDs pay. Riskier investments can drop just when you need the money.

Is the weekly amount exactly the same as monthly?

It’s the monthly amount × 12 ÷ 52, so it adds up to the same yearly total. Saving weekly instead of monthly can slightly speed things up because money starts earning sooner.

How can I reach my goal faster?

Automate a transfer on payday, put windfalls such as bonuses or tax refunds toward the goal, and raise your monthly saving whenever your income grows.
